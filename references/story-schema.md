# Story Schema

Every story in `story-bank.md` uses this exact structure. Consistency is what makes retrieval and rehearsal work later — do not improvise new headings.

```markdown
## S03 — [Short memorable title, 2-5 words]

**Chemistry:** High-stakes | Vulnerable | Humorous  (may be two, list primary first)
**Settings:** interview, client-call, leadership, networking, talk
**Length:** 45s / 90s / 3min
**Confidence:** verified | approximate | unverified

### Five anchors
1. [beat one]
2. [beat two]
3. [beat three]
4. [beat four]
5. [beat five]

### Point
[One sentence. The claim the story proves. This is what you say first when you use it.]

### Situation
[1-2 sentences. Only enough context to feel the tension. Who, where, when, what was at stake.]

### Tension
[What made this hard. The thing that could have gone wrong. Without this the story is a report, not a story.]

### Action
[What *you* personally did. Decisions, not chronology. Use "I" for your moves, "we" for team outcomes.]

### Turn
[The moment it changed. One line. This is the beat people remember.]

### Result
[What changed — for the user, the product, the business, the team. Numbers where they exist.]

### Lesson
[The transferable principle. One sentence, said as a belief you now hold.]

### Vivid details
- [A concrete image, a line of dialogue, a number, a sensory detail. 2-4 items.]

### Opens with
> "[The exact first sentence, written out. Not a script for the whole story — just the launch.]"

### Lands on
> "[The exact closing line. Do not let the story fade out.]"

### Use it when
- [Question, prompt, or moment that should trigger this story]
- [...]

### Do not claim
- [Anything the storyteller could not defend if challenged. Leave empty if nothing.]
```

## Field rules

**Title** — nameable in conversation ("the draft mode one"). Never a full sentence.

**ID** — `S01`, `S02`... assigned in order of capture, never renumbered. Retrieval and rehearsal reference these.

**Five anchors** — the memorization unit. Five beats, each under ten words. If the teller remembers the five anchors they can rebuild the story naturally without sounding recited. Never write a word-for-word script for the middle of the story; scripts sound memorized. Only the opening and closing lines are written verbatim.

**Point** — must be a claim, not a topic. Not "a time I handled conflict" but "the disagreement dissolved once we stopped arguing opinions and went and got evidence."

**Turn** — many people skip this. Push for it. Every usable story has a moment where the situation flips: the thing they discovered, the thing someone said, the decision made at the worst moment.

**Vivid details** — the difference between a story that is understood and one that is remembered. At least one concrete image or exact quote per story. "We were three days out and the build was still red" beats "we were behind schedule."

**Confidence** — mark `approximate` for remembered-but-unmeasured numbers and `unverified` for anything the teller cannot back up. Populate **Do not claim** with the overreach they might be tempted into. This protects them in a room where someone probes.

**Length** — the same story compressed to three sizes. 45s for a recruiter or a passing remark, 90s for a behavioural answer, 3min for a talk or a deep dive. Record which lengths the story actually works at; not every story survives compression to 45s.

## The index file

`story-index.md` is the fast-lookup companion. Regenerate it whenever stories are added or edited.

```markdown
# Story Index

_Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD · 12 stories_

## By situation
**Conflict / disagreement** → S01, S05, S09
**Failure / what you got wrong** → S03
**Influence without authority** → S01, S05
...

## By chemistry
**High-stakes** → S01, S03, S04
**Vulnerable** → S03, S07, S11
**Humorous** → S06, S08

## By setting
**Job interview** → S01–S12
**Client / sales call** → S02, S04, S07
**Leadership / team** → S05, S09, S11
**Networking / rapport** → S06, S07, S08

## Coverage gaps
- No humorous story yet — worth capturing one.
- Only one failure story; a second gives range.
```

Coverage gaps are the most valuable part of the index. Always compute them honestly: an unbalanced bank fails the moment an unexpected prompt lands.
