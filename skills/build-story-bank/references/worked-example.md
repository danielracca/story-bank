# Worked Example

An illustrative story entry showing the target quality bar. The details are invented — never copy them into a user's bank, only match the level of specificity.

---

## S04 — The Empty Dashboard

**Chemistry:** High-stakes, Vulnerable
**Settings:** interview, client-call, talk
**Length:** 45s / 90s / 3min
**Confidence:** approximate

### Five anchors
1. Six months building the analytics dashboard.
2. Launch week: nobody opened it twice.
3. Watched one customer use it live.
4. She wanted one number, not forty.
5. Cut it to one screen, usage tripled.

### Point
We built forty charts because we could, and the customer only ever wanted one number.

### Situation
We shipped an analytics dashboard after six months of work. In launch week the usage data was flat — people opened it once and never came back.

### Tension
The whole quarter had been spent on it, and it was the thing I had argued hardest for. Admitting it had missed meant admitting my own call had been wrong in front of the people I had convinced.

### Action
Instead of guessing, I asked five customers to open it while I watched and say what they were looking for. I did not demo anything. I just watched.

### Turn
The third one scrolled straight past every chart, found a single number at the bottom, screenshotted it, and pasted it into Slack. That was the entire job.

### Result
We cut the dashboard to one screen built around that number and the two things that moved it. Weekly active use went from about 8% of accounts to just over 25% within two months, and support tickets about "where do I find" dropped off almost entirely.

### Lesson
I now find out what people are going to do with the output before I decide what to build.

### Vivid details
- Forty-one charts. We counted them when we deleted them.
- She said "I just need the number for standup" without looking up.
- The screenshot-and-paste move — three customers did the identical thing.

### Opens with
> "We spent six months building something people used once."

### Lands on
> "That's why the first question I ask now is what they're going to do with it, not what they want to see."

### Use it when
- Tell me about a time you got something wrong.
- Tell me about a time user research changed a product.
- How do you decide what to build?
- Tell me about a time you had to admit a mistake.
- How do you define an MVP?
- What is something you believe now that you didn't before?
- Client call: when a prospect asks for a long feature list.
- Talk: opening story about building the wrong thing.

### Do not claim
- That I predicted the failure in advance. I did not; I argued for the build.
- Exact usage percentages beyond "roughly tripled" — the numbers are remembered, not sourced.

---

## What makes this entry work

- The **Point** is a claim with a number in it, not a topic.
- The **Turn** is a specific human action — the screenshot-and-paste — not a realisation.
- The **Result** has a before and after, and the confidence field admits the numbers are approximate.
- **Do not claim** protects against the version of the story that sounds better but is not true.
- **Use it when** spans three settings, so retrieval will find it from more than one direction.
- Nothing between the opening and closing lines is scripted. The five anchors carry it.
