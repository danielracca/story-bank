# Situation Map

What a room is actually asking for, and which story shape answers it. Use to translate a surface question into the underlying need before matching.

---

## Job interview

| They ask | They are really testing | Story shape |
|---|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | Can you compress and choose? | Arc, not history. Three moves and why you are here. |
| Tell me about a conflict | Do you handle disagreement like an adult? | Disagreement resolved by evidence or reframing, not by winning. |
| Tell me about a failure | Are you honest, and did you actually learn? | Real cost, owned without collapse, changed behaviour after. |
| Biggest accomplishment | What is your ceiling? | High-stakes, measurable, your specific contribution clear. |
| Time you influenced without authority | Can you operate above your title? | You changed a decision you did not own. |
| Hardest decision | Do you understand trade-offs? | Two good options, you chose, you can name what you gave up. |
| Why are you leaving | Are you a risk? | Forward-facing, no blame, honest about the constraint. |
| Do you have questions for us | Are you evaluating us too? | Not a story — but a question shaped by one. |

**Seniority shift.** For staff, principal, and lead roles, every answer should climb one level: not "I designed this" but "this was the right problem because, and here is what it changed for teams beyond mine."

## Client & sales calls

| Moment | Need | Story shape |
|---|---|---|
| Opening / warm-up | Lower defences | Humorous, 30 seconds, self-deprecating. |
| Discovery | Show you have seen their problem before | Someone like them, same pain, named accurately. |
| Objection | De-risk | A customer who had the same doubt and what happened. |
| Pricing pushback | Reframe to value | Cost of not solving it, from a real case. |
| Losing momentum | Re-establish stakes | High-stakes story where delay cost something. |
| Closing | Make the decision feel safe | Result story with a specific number. |

Never name a client without permission. Anonymise by shape: size, industry, stage.

## Leading a team

| Moment | Need | Story shape |
|---|---|---|
| New team, first week | Be trusted quickly | Vulnerable — a mistake you made and what it taught you. |
| Delivering hard news | Be believed | High-stakes, honest, no spin. |
| Someone is stuck | Normalise the struggle | A time you were stuck and what unstuck you. |
| Pushing for a standard | Make it concrete | A time the shortcut cost you. |
| Celebrating | Make it specific | Name the exact moment someone made the difference. |

## Networking & rapport

| Moment | Need | Story shape |
|---|---|---|
| "So what do you do?" | Be interesting in 20 seconds | The concrete example, never the job title. |
| Small talk stalling | Restart | Humorous, low stakes, invites a swap. |
| Meeting someone senior | Be memorable, not needy | One vivid detail from real work, then a question. |
| Reconnecting | Give them a reason to care | What has changed since, in one story. |

The rule that separates rapport from performance: end with a question. A story told at someone is a monologue. A story that hands back is a conversation.

---

## Talks & presentations

| Slot | Purpose | Story shape |
|---|---|---|
| Open | Buy attention | High-stakes or humorous. Never a preamble. |
| After a dense section | Reset the room | Short, human, light. |
| The core argument | Make the abstract concrete | Your strongest evidence story. |
| Close | Give them something to carry | Vulnerable, landing on the lesson. |

---

## Reading the room live

- **Short questions, fast follow-ups** → they want compression. Cut to the 45-second version.
- **They lean in and ask "then what?"** → they want the long version. Give the 3-minute one.
- **They interrupt with a related example** → stop; they are now in the conversation. Hand back.
- **Arms crossed, sceptical** → drop the impressive story and use the vulnerable one. Trust before proof.
- **Someone probes a number** → say what you actually know, and where the number came from. Never defend a figure you cannot source; the recovery costs less than the catch.
